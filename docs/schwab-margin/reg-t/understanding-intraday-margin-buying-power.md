# Understanding Intraday Margin Buying Power

Intraday margin buying power offers more flexibility for traders to open and close positions during the trading day. To use effectively, there are risks and rules to understand.

## Key takeaways

- Intraday margin buying power tracks, in real time, the sum of securities that a trader with an eligible margin account may trade intraday.
- Intraday margin buying power will be lower than the displayed value if a security has a margin requirement higher than 25%.
- It's important to review the security's margin requirement and resulting buying power before placing a trade.
- If a trader uses intraday margin buying power and overspends their available funds for trading, they may be issued a margin call unless they cover their deficiency by 8 p.m. ET.

Intraday margin buying power gives eligible margin traders more flexibility to open and close positions during the trading day. Understanding how the balance is calculated, how a trade affects it, and what must be covered by 8 p.m. ET can help traders avoid an unexpected margin call.

## What is intraday margin buying power?

Schwab calculates intraday margin buying power by dividing a trader's maintenance excess—the equity in an account above the maintenance requirement—by 0.25, equivalent to multiplying it by 4. The result is the dollar value of eligible stock the trader can trade intraday, assuming a standard 25% margin requirement. This dynamic balance rises or falls as trades execute, market values change, or positions become concentrated.

## Why is intraday margin buying power limited on some securities?

Not all stocks will be held at the standard 25% requirement. Requirements may differ based on volatility, liquidity, and several other risk factors. Let's look at examples where a trader with \$10,000 in maintenance excess (the account equity that exceeds the minimum maintenance requirements) considers two stocks:

- **Example 1:** QRS trades at \$50 per share and has a 25% margin requirement. Schwab platforms always assume a 25% requirement, so \$10,000/0.25, or \$40,000, will be displayed as intraday margin buying power. The only calculation the trader must do is intraday margin buying power divided by price per share to see how many shares they can purchase:
  - At \$50 per share, the trader can buy 800 shares (\$40,000/\$50) of QRS.\

- **Example 2: **ZYX trades at \$50 per share but has a 35% margin requirement. Because this requirement is higher than the standard, the trader must manually calculate intraday margin buying power *before* determining how many shares to purchase:
  - Intraday margin buying power for ZYX is \$10,000/0.35, or \$28,571.43.
  - At \$50 per share, the trader can buy 571 shares (\$28,571.43/\$50) of ZYX.

The trader can buy fewer shares of ZYX than QRS because ZYZ has a higher margin requirement.

## What happens if stock buying power is exceeded?

Consider an account with \$7,000 of available cash for trading, \$10,000 of maintenance excess, \$14,000 of stock buying power, and \$40,000 of intraday margin buying power. If the trader buys \$20,000 of QRS (held at 25%), the trade will be accepted because it is within the intraday margin buying power balance.

Stock buying power would fall to negative \$6,000 while intraday margin buying power remains positive at \$20,000. If the trader does not correct this negative stock buying power by 8 p.m. ET, they will be issued a Reg T (or Regulation T) call.

## Bottom line: Flexibility also requires discipline

Intraday margin buying power allows for flexibility in day trading. Traders should remember that this is an "intraday" balance that will not carry overnight and should fund their account or reduce positions before 8 p.m. ET when needed to avoid a margin call. It's important to monitor balances, check a security's margin requirement on Schwab.com, and use the thinkorswim order preview before placing any trade.
